How to see your speaker notes while sharing your screen

On a video call you want your notes in front of you, but not on your audience's screen. Here are four ways to do it on Windows, and when each one fits.

Published 26 November 2026 · Updated 28 November 2026 · ScreenCue by PeteStudioz.ai

Short answer: With two screens, use PowerPoint's Presenter View and share only the slideshow screen. In Microsoft Teams, share your slides with PowerPoint Live, which shows your notes only to you. With one screen and any meeting app, use a teleprompter that Windows leaves out of screen sharing, such as ScreenCue.

1. Two screens: PowerPoint's Presenter View

If you have a second monitor, PowerPoint's Presenter View shows your notes, the next slide and a timer on one screen and the slideshow on the other. In your meeting app, share only the screen with the slideshow.

Good when: you present from a desk with two displays.

2. Microsoft Teams: PowerPoint Live

In a Teams meeting, you can share a presentation with PowerPoint Live instead of sharing your screen. You see your slides with your notes, and attendees see only the slides. See Microsoft's guide to PowerPoint Live.

Good when: every talk you give is in Teams.

3. Notes on paper or another device

Print your notes pages, or open the presentation on a tablet or phone beside you. Nothing extra appears on your screen, but you look away from the camera and keep your place by hand.

Good when: you only need a few prompts.

4. One screen, any meeting app: a teleprompter hidden from screen sharing

ScreenCue puts your notes in a window over your slides. Windows leaves that window out of screen capture, so you can share your whole screen and your audience sees only your slides. With Sync & control PowerPoint on, the notes change with each slide.

Good when: you present from a laptop, or want your notes right next to your camera.

Set up ScreenCue for a video call

  1. Install ScreenCue from the Microsoft Store.
  2. Open your presentation (.pptx) in ScreenCue. It lists the speaker notes slide by slide.
  3. Select Open teleprompter. Drag it close to your webcam so you keep eye contact, then set the text size and background opacity.
  4. Turn on Sync & control PowerPoint, then start the slideshow in PowerPoint.
  5. In your meeting app, share your screen. Turn on click-through if you want clicks to pass through the teleprompter.
  6. Present with your clicker, the arrow keys or Page Up/Down. The notes change with each slide.

To close a click-through teleprompter, press Ctrl + Alt + Shift + Esc.

Check before you present

  • Hiding the teleprompter needs Windows 10 version 2004 or later, or Windows 11. On earlier versions of Windows 10, a black box may appear in its place.
  • Run a short test call, or record your screen once, and check what your audience sees.
  • A camera pointed at your screen, or a hardware capture device, still shows the teleprompter.
