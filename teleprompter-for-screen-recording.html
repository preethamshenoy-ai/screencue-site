How to read a script while recording your screen on Windows

Published 28 November 2026 · ScreenCue by PeteStudioz.ai

Short answer: Put your script in a window that Windows leaves out of screen capture. ScreenCue opens a Word, text or Markdown script in an always-on-top teleprompter that you see while you record, but that doesn't appear in the recording (Windows 10 version 2004 or later, or Windows 11). If you're recording a PowerPoint presentation inside PowerPoint, its own Record feature also has a Teleprompter view.

Why the script stays out of the recording

Windows lets an app mark a window as "exclude from capture". The window still shows on your monitor, but Windows leaves it out of screenshots, screen recordings and screen sharing. Microsoft documents this as window display affinity, available since Windows 10 version 2004. ScreenCue's teleprompter uses it, so your recording shows whatever is behind the teleprompter.

Set it up

  1. Write your script in Word (.docx), a text file (.txt) or Markdown (.md).
  2. In ScreenCue, select Open Word or text and choose the script. It opens as one cue.
  3. Select Open teleprompter. Place it near your webcam if you're on camera, or beside the area you're recording.
  4. Set the text size and background opacity. Keep click-through off, so you can scroll the script as you go.
  5. Start your screen recorder and record. Your script stays on your screen, not in the video.

Recording slides? Open the presentation with Open PowerPoint instead, and turn on Sync & control PowerPoint so the notes change with each slide.

Tips for reading a script naturally

  • Break the script into short lines at natural pauses, so you can find your place after you look away.
  • Keep the teleprompter narrow and close to the camera. The less your eyes move, the less it looks like you're reading.
  • Do a 10-second test recording first and play it back to check the teleprompter isn't in it.
  • Read at your own pace. ScreenCue doesn't scroll by itself, so you never have to chase the text.

Questions

Does ScreenCue scroll the script automatically?

No. You scroll it yourself, at your own pace. Keep click-through off while you need to scroll.

Will the teleprompter show in my recording?

Not in screen recordings on Windows 10 version 2004 or later and Windows 11. On earlier versions of Windows 10, a black box may appear in its place. A camera pointed at your screen, or a hardware capture device, would still record it.

Does it work with my screen recorder?

ScreenCue doesn't plug into any recorder: Windows leaves the teleprompter out of screen capture. Make a short test recording with the app you use to confirm.
